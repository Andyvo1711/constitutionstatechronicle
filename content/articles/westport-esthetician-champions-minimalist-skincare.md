Inside a converted carriage house just off the Post Road, Linden & Co. Skin Studio looks nothing like the multi-step skincare culture that dominates social media. There are no shelves crowded with serums, no elaborate twelve-step routines printed on laminated cards. Founder Priya Nandakumar has built her practice around a single, deliberately unfashionable idea: most people are using too much on their skin, not too little.

"I spend half of every consultation talking clients out of products," Nandakumar said, seated in the studio's sunlit treatment room. "People come in with a bag of six serums and ask which order to layer them in. My first question is usually whether they need all six at all."

## Rethinking the Routine

Nandakumar, who trained as an esthetician in New York before opening her Westport studio four years ago, built her business around what she calls "diagnostic facials," ninety-minute sessions that begin with a detailed skin analysis under magnification before any product touches the client's face. The goal, she said, is to identify the two or three interventions that will actually move the needle, rather than layering on treatments that may counteract each other.

The approach has resonated with a client base that Nandakumar describes as "skincare-fatigued," many of whom arrive having tried dozens of products marketed through influencer campaigns. Client Elena Roswick, a Westport marketing executive, said she came to Linden & Co. after years of trial and error with drugstore and prestige brands alike.

"I was doing so much that my skin never had a chance to actually respond to anything," Roswick said. "Priya cut my routine down to three products. It sounds counterintuitive, but my skin has never looked better."

## Balancing Simplicity With Modern Treatments

Nandakumar's minimalism does not extend to the equipment in her treatment room, which includes LED therapy panels and a microcurrent device she uses selectively for clients focused on skin firmness. She said the studio's philosophy is not about avoiding technology but about using it with restraint.

"There's a difference between a tool that supports the skin's own repair process and a routine that just piles on more inputs," she said. "I want every step in a treatment to have a clear reason for being there."

The studio has also begun offering quarterly "skin audits," a service in which returning clients revisit their product regimen every three months to assess what is still working. Nandakumar said the sessions often result in clients removing products rather than adding them, a reversal she considers a mark of success rather than lost revenue.

As Westport's wellness corridor has grown more competitive, with several new medical spas opening along the Post Road in the past year, Nandakumar said she has no plans to expand her menu to compete on volume. She currently sees a limited number of clients each week and has kept her staff to two additional estheticians, both of whom she trained personally.

"I would rather have twenty clients who trust the process than two hundred who are shopping for the next trend," she said. Linden & Co. Skin Studio is currently booking consultations for late spring, with a waitlist for its most popular diagnostic facial slots.
