At Willow Grove Day Spa in Danbury, the front desk no longer looks like a front desk. Gone is the tall counter that once separated staff from arriving clients, replaced by a low, rounded table where receptionists sit at eye level with guests checking in. The redesign, completed last year, was not purely aesthetic. Owner Camille Petrov said it reflects a broader rethinking of how the spa treats the moments before and after a treatment.

"We used to spend all of our training energy on what happens inside the treatment room," Petrov said. "But the client's experience actually starts the second they walk through the door, and it doesn't end until they're back in their car. We were leaving a lot of that experience unmanaged."

## Retraining the Entire Front-of-House Team

Petrov, who has owned Willow Grove for nine years, brought in a hospitality consultant last year to retrain her front-of-house staff, a group she said had previously received minimal formal training beyond how to operate the booking software. The new training covers everything from how to phrase questions during intake to how to manage a client's wait if a treatment is running behind schedule.

"There's a real skill to telling someone their appointment is running ten minutes late without making them feel like an afterthought," Petrov said. "We used to just kind of hope staff figured that out on their own. Now we actually teach it."

The spa has also changed its post-treatment process, adding a brief check-in conducted by front desk staff, rather than solely the treating esthetician or massage therapist, to gather feedback and address any concerns before a client leaves. Front desk lead Marguerite Simeon, who has worked at Willow Grove for four years, said the new approach has changed how she sees her own role.

"I used to think of myself as basically a scheduler," Simeon said. "Now I feel like I'm part of the actual client experience. That's a different kind of job, honestly, and it's more satisfying."

## Measuring the Impact on Client Retention

Petrov said she began tracking client retention more closely after the changes and has seen a modest but consistent increase in repeat bookings among clients who had a first visit within the past year. She attributes part of that shift to the improved front-of-house experience, though she acknowledged it is difficult to isolate the effect of a single change.

Client Priscilla Odom, who began visiting Willow Grove last spring, said the atmosphere at check-in was part of what convinced her to return. "The girl at the front desk remembered my name and asked how a specific stress issue I'd mentioned last time was doing," Odom said. "That kind of detail makes you feel like more than just a number on a schedule."

Petrov said she is now working on formalizing a similar training program for phone-based client interactions, an area she believes remains underdeveloped across the spa industry more broadly. "The treatment itself might be excellent," she said, "but if everything around it feels transactional, clients don't come back. We learned that the hard way."
