At Meridian Aesthetics, a medical spa near downtown Hartford, medical director Dr. Sofia Palermo said her appointment book fills up roughly six weeks in advance for popular injectable treatments, a shift from just two years ago when same-week bookings were routine. The change reflects a broader surge in demand for non-surgical cosmetic procedures that has reshaped how the practice operates.

"We used to be able to accommodate walk-in consultations pretty regularly," Palermo said. "Now we're scheduling new patient consultations weeks out, and that's created real pressure on how we manage the practice day to day."

## A Changing Client Base

Palermo, a board-certified physician who transitioned from primary care to aesthetic medicine six years ago, said the demographic seeking treatment at Meridian has broadened considerably. Where the practice's early clients skewed toward women in their forties and fifties seeking treatment for fine lines, Palermo now regularly sees clients in their late twenties and early thirties inquiring about preventative injectable treatments, as well as a growing number of male clients.

"The stigma has decreased significantly, especially among younger clients," Palermo said. "But that also means we have to be more careful about managing expectations, because a twenty-eight-year-old asking about preventative treatment has very different needs than a client in her fifties addressing established lines."

To manage the growing patient volume responsibly, Meridian has added a second injector to its staff, nurse practitioner Colette Renaud, who completed an extensive training and mentorship period under Palermo before beginning to treat patients independently. Palermo said she has resisted pressure to expand more quickly, citing concerns about patient safety in an industry that has seen rapid, sometimes poorly regulated growth nationally.

## Balancing Growth With Safety Standards

Connecticut requires medical spas offering injectable treatments to operate under physician oversight, a regulatory structure Palermo said she takes seriously even as some competitors in the region have pushed the boundaries of that oversight. She said she personally reviews treatment plans for every new patient at Meridian, regardless of which injector will perform the procedure.

"There are practices out there where a physician's name is on the license but they've never actually seen the patient," Palermo said. "That's not how we operate here, and I think patients are increasingly asking the right questions about who is actually involved in their care."

Patient Naomi Whitcombe, who began treatment at Meridian last year for early signs of aging around her eyes, said the thoroughness of her initial consultation was part of what convinced her to choose the practice over a lower-cost competitor.

"They spent almost an hour with me before we even discussed a treatment plan," Whitcombe said. "That felt different from what I'd read about at some other places."

Palermo said Meridian is now exploring whether to add body contouring treatments to its menu, a category of non-invasive procedures that has seen significant growth nationally, though she said any expansion would proceed only after additional staff training and equipment evaluation. "Growth for its own sake isn't the goal," she said. "Growth that keeps pace with what we can safely deliver is."
