Southern Connecticut State University is deepening its partnerships with New Haven public schools, placing education students in city classrooms earlier and more frequently than the university's traditional teacher preparation pathway required in past years. Faculty overseeing the initiative say the goal is to give aspiring teachers sustained exposure to urban classrooms well before their formal student teaching placements begin.

Under the expanded partnership, first- and second-year education majors now spend regular hours each semester volunteering or observing in New Haven public school classrooms, a departure from a model that previously reserved most classroom time for upperclassmen completing required practicum hours.

## Learning the Realities of Urban Classrooms Early

Faculty who helped design the expanded partnership say early exposure serves two purposes: helping students confirm whether teaching is the right career path before investing years in a degree program, and giving them a more realistic sense of the particular challenges and rewards of teaching in an urban district, where classrooms often serve students facing a wide range of socioeconomic circumstances.

"We were finding that some students didn't fully grapple with what urban teaching involves until their final semester, when it was almost too late to make an informed decision," said an education faculty member involved in the partnership. "Getting them into classrooms earlier gives them a much more honest picture."

## A Two-Way Partnership

New Haven school administrators say the arrangement benefits their schools as well, providing extra hands in classrooms and a pipeline of potential future hires who already have direct experience with the district's schools, students, and academic priorities. A district administrator involved in coordinating the partnership said familiarity with a school's specific culture and student population often makes a meaningful difference in how quickly a new teacher can become effective in the classroom.

Several New Haven principals have also noted that the partnership gives current teachers additional support in classrooms that might otherwise be short-staffed for certain activities, such as small-group instruction or one-on-one reading support, without requiring the district to hire additional paraprofessionals.

## Student Perspectives on Early Placement

Education students who have participated in the earlier placements describe the experience as clarifying, for better or worse. One sophomore education major said her first semester volunteering in a New Haven elementary classroom confirmed her decision to pursue teaching, despite witnessing firsthand some of the resource challenges the school faced. "It didn't scare me off, it made me want to be part of the solution," she said.

Not every student comes away with the same conclusion, and faculty say that is, in part, the point. Some students who initially declared education as a major have used early classroom exposure to realize the profession was not the right fit for them, allowing them to change academic direction earlier rather than after completing a full teacher preparation sequence.

## Looking Toward Long-Term Impact

University officials say they hope the deepened partnership will, over time, translate into a stronger pipeline of new teachers choosing to begin their careers in New Haven schools specifically, addressing a persistent challenge many urban districts face in attracting early-career teachers with meaningful prior classroom exposure. The university has indicated it plans to explore similar early-placement partnerships with additional districts in the greater New Haven area as funding and staffing allow.
