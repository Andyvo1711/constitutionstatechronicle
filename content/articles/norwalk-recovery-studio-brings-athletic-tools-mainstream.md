For years, tools like compression boots and percussion massage guns were the province of professional locker rooms and elite training facilities. At Reset Recovery Lab, tucked into a light-industrial building near Norwalk's rail yard, co-owners Marcus Delgado and Whitney Faraday have built a business around bringing those same tools to office workers, weekend runners, and parents chasing after small children.

"You don't have to be training for a marathon to benefit from proper recovery," Delgado said. "Most of our clients are just people whose bodies are worn down from ordinary life, whether that's a desk job or chasing a toddler around all day."

## Making Recovery Accessible

Delgado, a former college strength and conditioning coach, and Faraday, a licensed physical therapist, opened Reset Recovery Lab two years ago after noticing a gap between high-performance athletic facilities and the general fitness market. The studio offers drop-in sessions using compression sleeves, percussion massage devices, and infrared sauna pods, alongside guided mobility classes led by staff trained in corrective exercise.

"A lot of people had never even heard of some of this equipment before it started showing up on social media," Faraday said. "Our job is to explain what actually helps versus what's just trendy, and to make sure people are using the tools correctly."

The studio's membership model allows clients to book individual recovery sessions or combine them with guided mobility classes, an approach Faraday said reflects the reality that most recovery tools work best alongside, not instead of, targeted movement work.

## A Client Base Beyond Elite Athletes

While Reset Recovery Lab does count several competitive runners and a handful of semi-professional athletes among its clients, Delgado said the bulk of the business comes from people managing everyday physical stress. Office worker Devon Kowalczyk, who started coming to the studio after months of lower back tightness from long hours at a desk, said he was skeptical at first that the equipment would offer more than a temporary fix.

"I thought it might just feel good in the moment and then wear off," Kowalczyk said. "But combined with the mobility work they had me doing, I noticed an actual change in how my back feels by the end of a work week."

The studio has also begun partnering with a handful of local employers to offer discounted membership packages as part of workplace wellness benefits, a strategy Delgado said has helped smooth out slower weekday afternoons when demand from individual clients tends to dip.

Faraday said the biggest challenge has been managing client expectations around recovery tools that are sometimes marketed online with exaggerated claims. "We spend a lot of time just being honest about what these tools can and can't do," she said. "A percussion massager isn't going to fix a structural problem. But used well, it can be part of a much bigger picture of taking care of your body."

Reset Recovery Lab currently operates seven days a week and is planning to add a second infrared sauna pod later this year to accommodate growing demand from its expanding membership base.
