On a quiet stretch of Greenwich Avenue, tucked behind a discreet brass door, Belle Rivage Spa has spent the past several months reworking nearly every treatment on its menu. The changes are subtle to a passerby but significant to regulars: ninety-minute massages have replaced the standard hour, facials now begin with a fifteen-minute scalp ritual, and the spa's signature "Sound Bath Soak" pairs a copper tub with a live singing-bowl session in an adjoining alcove.

"Our clients aren't looking for a quick fix anymore," said Belle Rivage founder Adrienne Castellano, who opened the spa eleven years ago after a career in hospitality management in New York. "They want to disappear for an afternoon. That means we had to rethink the pacing of everything we offer, not just add a new service to a brochure."

## A Shift Toward Slower Rituals

The redesign reflects a broader trend playing out across Fairfield County's luxury spa scene, where operators say clients increasingly prize unhurried, multi-sensory experiences over the express treatments that dominated the previous decade. Castellano said the spa's most requested addition is the "Three Hour Escape," a bundled package combining a hydrating body wrap, a lymphatic drainage massage, and a private tea service in the spa's greenhouse-style solarium.

Pricing for the new packages runs higher than the old menu, a decision Castellano acknowledged was not without risk. "We lost a few clients who wanted convenience," she said. "But we gained clients who wanted depth. That trade has been worth it for us."

Staff training has also expanded. Belle Rivage now requires its estheticians to complete an additional forty hours of instruction in lymphatic techniques and aromatherapy blending before they can perform the flagship treatments, a standard Castellano says exceeds Connecticut's licensing minimums.

## Local Competition and Client Loyalty

The changes come as Greenwich's wellness market grows more crowded, with several boutique studios opening within walking distance over the past two years. Longtime client Marguerite Voss, a retired attorney who has visited Belle Rivage monthly since 2019, said the new format has deepened her loyalty rather than tested it.

"I used to come in for an hour and leave feeling like I'd barely exhaled," Voss said. "Now I block off the whole afternoon. It changes how you experience the treatment entirely."

Castellano said she is also weighing whether to introduce a members-only tier that would offer priority booking and access to seasonal treatments tied to local ingredients, including a cranberry and sea salt scrub sourced from a farm in nearby Stamford. No launch date has been set, but she said the idea has come up repeatedly in client surveys conducted this winter.

For now, the spa is focused on refining what it already has. Castellano said she reviews client feedback weekly and has already adjusted the solarium tea service twice based on requests for more variety in herbal blends. "This industry rewards attentiveness," she said. "You cannot build a menu once and expect it to hold for a decade. Our clients are telling us what they need, and our job is to listen closely enough to hear it."

Belle Rivage Spa is open Tuesday through Sunday and currently books appointments up to six weeks in advance for its expanded treatment offerings.
