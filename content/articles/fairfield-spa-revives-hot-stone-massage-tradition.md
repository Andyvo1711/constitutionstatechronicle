At Stoneleigh Wellness in Fairfield, a tray of smooth basalt stones warms in a specialized heater in the corner of every treatment room, ready for what has become the spa's most requested service. In an industry often chasing the newest device or technology-driven treatment, owner and lead massage therapist Julienne Marchand has built her business around something considerably older: hot stone massage, a technique she says remains unmatched for certain kinds of muscular tension.

"There's a temptation in this industry to keep adding gadgets to justify higher prices," Marchand said. "But heat applied correctly, combined with skilled hands, does something that a machine simply can't replicate. I wanted to build a spa around getting that fundamental technique right, rather than chasing whatever is newest."

## Precision Behind a Traditional Technique

Marchand, who trained extensively in hot stone methodology early in her career before spending several years focused on more conventional deep tissue work, said she was drawn back to the technique after noticing how effectively it addressed chronic tension in clients who had not responded as well to standard massage alone. She said the technique requires far more skill and attention than its relaxing reputation might suggest, particularly around stone temperature control and the sequencing of stone placement relative to muscle groups.

"People assume hot stone massage is just a relaxation treatment, almost a luxury add-on," Marchand said. "Done properly, it's actually a precise therapeutic technique. The heat allows deeper tissue work with less discomfort than an equivalent deep tissue massage without stones."

Stoneleigh requires every therapist on staff to complete a dedicated hot stone certification program before offering the service independently, a standard Marchand said goes beyond what many spas require. She personally oversees the training for new hires, a process that typically takes several weeks of supervised practice before a therapist is cleared to work with clients unsupervised.

## A Loyal Following Built on Results

Client Desmond Halloway, a Fairfield contractor who deals with chronic shoulder and neck tension from years of physical work, said he had tried numerous massage approaches before finding consistent relief at Stoneleigh. "I was skeptical it would be different from a regular deep tissue massage," Halloway said. "The heat makes a real difference in how deep the therapist can work without it feeling painful."

Marchand said the spa's emphasis on a single, well-executed specialty has helped it stand out in a Fairfield County market crowded with spas offering sprawling treatment menus. She has resisted pressure to add trendier offerings like cupping or specialized facial devices, preferring to deepen the spa's expertise in its core technique rather than diluting focus across a wider range of services.

"I'd rather be known as the place that does one thing better than almost anyone else in the region," Marchand said, "than as a spa with twenty mediocre options on the menu." Stoneleigh Wellness currently operates five days a week and has begun offering extended ninety-minute sessions for clients managing chronic pain conditions, developed in consultation with a local physical therapy practice.
